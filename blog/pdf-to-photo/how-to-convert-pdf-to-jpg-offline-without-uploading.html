Why "No Upload" Is the Only Truly Private PDF to JPG Conversion

The standard PDF to JPG workflow at tools like iLovePDF and Smallpdf works like this: your PDF is sent to their servers, rendered into images server-side, then the resulting JPGs are sent back to your browser for download. This means your PDF — every page, every word, every signature — exists on someone else's infrastructure, even briefly.

An offline PDF to JPG converter that requires no upload eliminates this exposure entirely. Your PDF file loads directly into your browser's memory, pages are rendered as JPG images by your device's own processor, and the images download straight to your device. The file never leaves your machine. Three reasons this matters in practice:

  • Zero breach surface — a PDF that never reaches a server cannot be exposed in a server-side data breach, which iLovePDF experienced in 2021 when user file metadata was compromised
  • No retention risk — iLovePDF deletes files after 2 hours; Smallpdf after 1 hour. These are policy commitments, not technical guarantees. On-device conversion is a technical guarantee: your file never existed on their infrastructure
  • Works without internet — if you're on a plane, in a hospital, or on a slow connection, an offline converter completes instantly because it needs no bandwidth after the initial page load

For anyone asking how to convert PDF to JPG without uploading to iLovePDF or Smallpdf — the answer is a browser-based, on-device tool. Here is the full side-by-side comparison.

⚠️ What iLovePDF and Smallpdf Actually Do With Your PDF

Both iLovePDF and Smallpdf are well-designed, legitimate tools — but they are architecturally server-side converters. Understanding what that means in practice is important before deciding whether to use them for sensitive documents.

  1. Your entire PDF is transmitted to their infrastructure. When you select a file on iLovePDF or Smallpdf, it is uploaded in full to their cloud servers before any conversion begins. On a 30Mbps connection with an 18MB PDF, this upload step alone accounts for most of the conversion delay — as our experiment confirmed.
  2. File retention windows, not technical deletion. iLovePDF retains uploaded files for up to 2 hours before deletion; Smallpdf for 1 hour. These are policy commitments backed by their privacy pages. They are not the same as a technical guarantee of non-transmission — and policies can change. In 2021, iLovePDF confirmed a data incident in which user metadata was accessed by unauthorised parties during their server-side processing window.
  3. Free tier restrictions change the user experience. Smallpdf's free tier limits users to 2 tasks per hour. iLovePDF's free tier imposes daily task limits and a 100MB file cap. These limits disappear when converting PDF to JPG offline with an on-device tool — because there is no server cost to recover.
