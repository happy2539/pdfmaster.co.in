🖼️ The Core Difference Between JPG and PNG

Before deciding which image format produces a better PDF, you need to understand the fundamental technical difference between the two:

  • JPG (JPEG) uses lossy compression — it permanently discards some image data to achieve smaller file sizes. It excels at photographs with gradients, complex colour transitions, and natural scenes.
  • PNG uses lossless compression — every pixel is preserved exactly. It excels at screenshots, logos, diagrams, text-over-image, and any graphic requiring a transparent background.

When you embed either format in a PDF, the converter includes the image data as-is. This means the quality and file size of your final PDF is directly determined by the format you choose upfront. 📸 When JPG Is the Better Choice for PDF

JPG is the superior format for PDF when your primary goal is smaller file size with acceptable visual quality. Based on our test data, JPG outperforms PNG for PDF in these specific scenarios:

✅ Use JPG for PDF When…

  • Converting personal or professional photographs — portraits, landscapes, product shots
  • Creating photo albums or portfolios as PDF documents
  • File size matters — sending PDFs by email or WhatsApp
  • Images don't contain any text, sharp lines, or logos
  • Storage space is limited on your device

⚠️ Avoid JPG for PDF When…

  • The image contains typed or printed text
  • You're embedding logos, icons, or brand assets
  • The PDF will be printed professionally at high DPI
  • Images include charts, graphs, or UI screenshots
  • You need a transparent background in the PDF

🖥️ When PNG Is the Better Choice for PDF

PNG is the superior format for PDF whenever visual accuracy matters more than file size. Our experiment showed a dramatic quality difference between JPG and PNG for these content types:

  1. Screenshots and screen recordings — JPG compression introduces blurring and colour banding around UI elements and text labels. PNG preserves every pixel exactly as it appeared on screen.
  2. Infographics and data visualisations — bar charts, pie charts, and flow diagrams contain sharp colour boundaries that JPG's lossy compression visibly degrades, even at high quality settings.
  3. Logos and brand assets — for documents like proposals, invoices, and reports, a PNG logo in a PDF looks professional. The same logo as a JPG often appears slightly soft or develops a faint halo around edges.
  4. Documents with embedded text — if text is baked into the image (not PDF text layers), JPG compression makes it visibly harder to read. PNG maintains perfect legibility.

🔲 PNG Transparency in PDF: How It Works

One of the most important reasons to choose PNG over JPG for PDF is alpha-channel transparency. JPG simply cannot carry a transparent background — it always fills transparency with white or another solid colour. PNG's transparency behaves as follows when embedded in a PDF:

  • A PNG logo with a transparent background placed in a PDF will appear correctly over any page background colour — white, coloured, or even another image.
  • The PDF format natively supports transparency via its graphics model, so no quality is lost and no white box appears around the image.
  • The same PNG converted to JPG first will have a white (or black) rectangle behind it in the PDF — destroying the design.
⚠️
Common Mistake to Avoid

Many users convert their PNG logos to JPG to "reduce file size" before making a PDF. This permanently destroys the transparency and any future re-conversion back to PNG will not restore it. Always keep your master PNG files and embed them directly into your PDF — PDFMaster handles this correctly without any conversion step.

🏆 Final Verdict: JPG vs PNG — Which Is Better for PDF?

After testing 50 real-world images across both formats, the answer is clear — but it is not a single winner:

✅
Use JPG for PDF when your images are photographs.

JPG produces PDFs that are 3–4× smaller with no meaningful visual quality difference for natural photographic content. For photo albums, portfolios, and picture-heavy reports, JPG is the correct choice — your PDF will be compact, fast to share, and visually excellent.

✅
Use PNG for PDF when your images contain text, lines, or transparency.

PNG's lossless compression is the only correct choice for screenshots, diagrams, UI exports, logos, and scanned documents. JPG compression introduces visible artefacts on sharp edges — a problem that permanently affects your PDF quality and cannot be undone after conversion.
