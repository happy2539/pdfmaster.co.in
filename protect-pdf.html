Cryptographic Integrity

Why AES-256 PDF Encryption is Mathematically Impossible to Crack

PDFMaster utilizes standard AES-256 (Revision 6) encryption specified in ISO 32000-2 (PDF 2.0). Unlike legacy RC4 methods that can be vulnerable to key extraction, AES-256 uses a 256-bit symmetric encryption key derived with modern PBKDF2 and SHA-256 hashing algorithms.

With a 256-bit key, there are 1.15 × 1077 possible combinations. Even if the world’s top 1,000 supercomputers attempted billions of guesses every second, it would take longer than the age of the universe to brute-force a strong password.

  • Open (User) Password: Encrypts the entire document stream. Nobody can view, render, or inspect page contents without entering this password.
  • Permissions (Owner) Password: Locks permissions flags inside conforming readers (like Adobe Acrobat, Apple Preview, and modern browsers) to prevent printing, text copying, or extraction.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear answers about PDF security, password management, and document protection.

How strong is AES-256 PDF encryption?

AES-256 (Advanced Encryption Standard with a 256-bit key) is the cryptographic benchmark adopted by the U.S. federal government, military organizations, and international banking networks. With 2256 possible keys, it would take current supercomputers trillions of years to brute-force a properly chosen password. When you lock your PDF with AES-256 and a strong password, it is mathematically impossible to unlock without the password.

Are my files or passwords uploaded to a server?

Never. PDFMaster is architected from the ground up to operate 100% on the client side using WebAssembly and Web Crypto API. Your document data, metadata, and passwords never leave your computer or phone. You can even disconnect your internet before clicking Encrypt and the tool will continue to work perfectly.

Can anyone open my PDF without the password?

No. When you set an Open Password, the entire internal content stream of the PDF is scrambled with AES-256 encryption. Every standard PDF viewer (Adobe Acrobat, Apple Preview, Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, mobile viewers) will display a password prompt and reject access until the correct password is provided.

What happens if I forget my password?

Because true AES-256 encryption contains zero backdoors and PDFMaster does not store your password on any server, there is no way to reset or bypass a forgotten password. Always ensure you record your password in a trusted password manager before locking critical documents.

What is the difference between an Open Password and an Owner Password?

The Open (User) Password is required to open, decrypt, and view the PDF. The Owner (Permissions) Password is used to manage or remove security restrictions (like allowing printing or copying) without having to give recipients the power to change security settings.

Can I lock multiple files or very large PDFs?

Yes! Thanks to PDFMaster's Low-RAM architecture, large documents with hundreds of pages are encrypted smoothly in-memory without crashing your browser or eating all your device RAM.

Will the locked PDF work on mobile devices?

Yes. Encrypted PDFs created by PDFMaster adhere strictly to ISO 32000-2 standards and open seamlessly on iPhone (iOS Files, Apple Books), iPad, Android, macOS, Windows, and Linux.

Why does PDFMaster deliberately avoid saving sessions or caching passwords?

Saving passwords or confidential files in browser storage (like IndexedDB or localStorage) creates a dangerous security vulnerability on shared, corporate, or public computers. If colleagues, family members, or malicious browser extensions inspected your browser storage, your confidential document and password could be retrieved. PDFMaster is engineered with a strict Ephemeral Zero-Trace Policy: processing lives exclusively in volatile device RAM and is immediately destroyed the second you close or refresh the browser tab.
